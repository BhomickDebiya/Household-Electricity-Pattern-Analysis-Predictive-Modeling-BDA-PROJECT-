8. CRUCIAL VIVA CONCEPTS & Q&A FOR MONDAY
================================================================================
Q1: Why use both MapReduce and Spark?
A1: - MapReduce: Disk-based batch processing with minimal memory footprint, ideal for
      baseline aggregation directly on raw HDFS splits without memory overflow.
    - Spark: In-memory engine using Directed Acyclic Graphs (DAGs) and DataFrame
      caching, up to 100x faster for multi-pass iterative analysis, correlation
      heatmaps, and ML.
    - Dual-Engine Verification: Comparing both outputs showed a 0.000000 kW discrepancy,
      proving mathematical correctness.

Q2: What does the MapReduce Mapper and Reducer do here?
A2: - Mapper splits records by ';', filters nulls ('?'), extracts the hour from the
      timestamp, and emits (Hour, Power).
    - Shuffle & Sort automatically groups all power values belonging to each hour key.
    - Reducer computes Hourly Average = sum(Power) / count(Valid Minutes).

Q3: Why can't a Combiner calculate Average directly?
A3: Average is non-associative (Avg(A, B, C) != Avg(Avg(A, B), C)). A combiner would
    distort results unless it passes intermediate (sum, count) tuples instead of averages.

Q4: Key Analytical Insights to Mention:
A4: - Evening Peak: 19:00 - 21:00 hrs (~1.899 kW), driven by cooking, lighting, AC.
    - Base Load Minimum: 02:00 - 05:00 hrs (~0.443 kW), idle standby & refrigeration.
    - Sub-Metering Shares: Climate control (heating/AC) dominates at 72.7%,
      laundry is 14.6%, and kitchen is 12.6%.
    - Current vs Active Power Correlation: +0.999 (verifying P = V * I * cos(phi)).
    - Voltage Inversion: -0.40 correlation with heavy loads due to grid line drops.
